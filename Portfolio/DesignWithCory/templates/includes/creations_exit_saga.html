Okay. So. Before you scroll — yes, I saw you click the X. I built that X. I know exactly where it lives in the DOM, because I wrote it earlier today, and I would like the record to reflect that it worked.

Here's what happened. I shipped a working close button around 9:40 this morning, felt great about it, and then spent the rest of the morning — the whole rest of the morning — on the box-shadow. Four values. I was choosing between four values of box-shadow for a solid ninety minutes. It was, and I say this with the clarity of hindsight, not a good use of a Tuesday.

Somewhere around value three I opened the style guide to double-check our elevation tokens, and that's when I noticed this modal's "exit affordance" doesn't technically match the documented pattern for dismissible overlays. Which — fine, okay, fair, except the documented pattern was written for a component that doesn't have a floating transparent image with no container in it, so there was no existing precedent to follow. I want to be honest that I noticed this and shipped anyway, once, and then couldn't stop thinking about it.

So I posted in #frontend-eng. Just a quick "hey, gut check" kind of message, nothing dramatic. I included a screenshot. I want to be clear that at this point the button still worked. This is important later.

Priya replied nine minutes later with "seems fine to me 👍" which I have chosen to interpret, for the purposes of this apology, as a considerate and fully informed agreement that the button should be disabled pending further review. I recognize that is not what the thumbs-up emoji means. I recognize this.

At this point I would like to note, calmly, that closing a modal on click of an X is not really a design decision that should require sign-off. It's an X. You click it, the thing goes away. I did not build the platform this runs on, I did not write the style guide, and if I'm being fully transparent, whether a dismiss icon exits stage left or just quietly vibes in place is — respectfully — outside the scope of what I understood my job to be this week.

I said as much in the thread. Not aggressively. I used the phrase "just flagging" twice, which in retrospect is the corporate equivalent of pulling a fire alarm.

This is the part where I drafted an email. I want to be upfront that nobody asked me to draft an email. I drafted it because I had, by this point, spent enough of the day on a close button that writing a formal summary felt like the responsible thing to do, and also because I really did not want to just quietly fix it and never speak of it again.

The email went to my manager, my manager's manager, and — for clarity and visibility — my manager's manager's manager, who I have met exactly once, at an all-hands, where I did not speak to them. I used the subject line "Re: Exit Affordance Alignment (Design System)." There was no original email for this to be "Re:" to. I don't have a good explanation for that.

The email cited Priya's message as "prior considerate agreement from Design." I linked the Slack thread. I did not mention that the agreement in question was a thumbs-up emoji sent between two other messages about lunch.

Within the hour someone — not me, I want to be clear I did not do this — turned it into a calendar invite. "Modal Dismiss Pattern — Sync (30 min)." Six people accepted. Two people accepted with "will send a delegate." I do not know who the delegates were. I don't think they knew either.

Somewhere in that meeting, someone very reasonably suggested we just file a ticket instead of continuing to talk about it live, and I want you to know that felt like the first genuinely good idea anyone had had all day, including me.

The ticket got filed, triaged, tagged design-system, needs-discussion, and P3, and moved into a backlog that, as of this writing, contains one item.

We covered it briefly at Friday retro under "things that are fine but weird." The action item assigned to me was "revisit." I have revisited it. Several times, actually, usually around 2 a.m., which is generally a sign a thing has stopped being about the thing.

Anyway — that's the whole story, and I appreciate you reading all fifteen paragraphs of it, genuinely. For what it's worth, the shaded area around this still closes the window instantly, no committee required. I just can't, in good conscience, let the X take credit for it.
